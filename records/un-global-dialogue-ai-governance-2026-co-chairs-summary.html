Summary

Official summary of the inaugural UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance (Geneva, 6-7 July 2026, under resolution 79/325), with delegations from 163 countries. The Co-Chairs record convergence on four points: AI's promise must be weighed against risks and capacity built; divides in compute, data and skills shape who can govern AI; human rights, especially children's protection, apply across the life cycle; and no single actor can govern AI alone. The cluster on safe, secure and trustworthy AI found safety frameworks often lack evidence, called for a shared minimum baseline, and highlighted technical standards, open benchmarks, shared evaluation tools, third-party testing and incident reporting.

Status notes

A chair's summary, not a negotiated outcome: it records discussions and areas of convergence without binding commitments. The document is dated for the 6-7 July 2026 session; the PDF was created on 8 September 2026 and is filed under the UN site's 2026-09 folder, so September 2026 is used as the issue date. The Scientific Panel presented its Preliminary Report (UN-08) at the session. An AI Dialogue Partnerships Hub showcased over 350 commitments. The second Dialogue is scheduled for New York on 3-4 May 2027, back-to-back with the STI Forum. The BRICS AI statement (July 2025) committed its members to operationalising this Dialogue.
