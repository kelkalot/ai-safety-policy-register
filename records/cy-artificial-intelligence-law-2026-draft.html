Summary

Draft Cypriot law setting up the national framework for applying and supervising the EU AI Act, published for consultation by the Commissioner of Communications on 16 August 2026. Its 25 articles cover the competent authorities, the notifying authority and notification procedure, market surveillance, serious-incident notification, information and investigation powers, sanctions and collection of administrative fines, registration of high-risk AI systems, document retention, rights of appeal and complaint, an exception for real-time biometric identification, an AI regulatory sandbox and regulatory decisions. A companion bill creates an AI Regulation Directorate in the Commissioner's office.

Status notes

Consultation ran on the government's e-consultation platform until 16 September 2026 and drew 102 comments; the platform marks the bill as under processing and no bill has yet been tabled in the House of Representatives. The companion bill, the Electronic Communications and Postal Services Regulation (Amendment) Law of 2026, was consulted over the same period. In January 2025 the Council of Ministers designated the Commissioner of Communications as notifying authority, market surveillance authority and single point of contact, and the Commissioner for Personal Data Protection as a market surveillance authority within her remit, and created a national AI Taskforce chaired by the Chief Scientist. Press reports of the consultation describe maximum fines aligned with the AI Act (up to EUR 35 million or 7% of worldwide turnover for prohibited practices) and a role for the Central Bank of Cyprus for financial institutions. The Commission's list of 7 September 2026 shows the Commissioner of Communications for Cyprus with the designation marked as pending.
