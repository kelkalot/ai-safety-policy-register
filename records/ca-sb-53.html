Summary

First US state frontier-AI safety law. Large frontier developers must publish a frontier AI framework describing how they assess and mitigate catastrophic risks and how they draw on national and international standards, and must publish transparency reports for new models. Critical safety incidents must be reported to the Office of Emergency Services. The law protects whistleblowers, sets civil penalties enforced by the Attorney General, creates the CalCompute consortium, and requires annual update recommendations.

Status notes

Signed 29 Sep 2025; effective 1 Jan 2026. Applies to frontier models trained above 10^26 operations; most duties fall on 'large frontier developers' with over $500M in annual revenue. Penalties up to $1M per violation. Federal pressure: commentators named it a likely target under EO 14365, and the federal FRONTIER Act would preempt it. On 14 Sep 2026 Fortune reported an allegation that OpenAI's latest releases may not comply (secondary source). Governor's EO N-9-26 (18 Sep 2026) seeks recommendations by 16 Nov 2026 on strengthening state law: independent verification of filings, onsite auditors, a 'kill switch', and loss-of-control incidents. Not amended as of verification. Incident reports are due within 15 days, or 24 hours where there is imminent risk of death or serious injury.
