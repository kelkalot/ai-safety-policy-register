Summary

Would have created an 'Artificial Intelligence Bill of Rights'. Companion chatbot platforms would have had to bar minors without parental consent, give parents controls and self-harm alerts, and take reasonable measures against material harmful to minors. Bot operators would have had to tell users hourly they are not talking to a human, and AI companies could have sold or disclosed personal information only as deidentified data. Commercial use of AI-generated likenesses would have needed consent, governments could not have contracted for AI with entities tied to foreign countries of concern, and schools could not have given AI instructional tools to pupils below grade 6, with exceptions.

Status notes

Filed on 22 Dec 2025 and referred to Commerce and Tourism and to Appropriations. Commerce and Tourism reported it favourably (10-0) on 21 Jan 2026. Appropriations adopted a committee substitute (18-0) on 18 Feb 2026 that added, among other changes, a 45-day cure period, a news-media exception for AI-generated likenesses and the rules on AI instructional tools in schools. On 4 Mar 2026 the Senate adopted two floor amendments and passed the bill 35-2. It died in House messages on 13 Mar 2026 without becoming law; the similar House bill HB 1395 died in the Information Technology Budget & Policy Subcommittee the same day. The bill stated an effective date of 1 Jul 2026. As passed by the Senate, the Department of Legal Affairs would have enforced the chatbot, bot and data provisions as deceptive or unfair trade practices, with civil penalties of up to USD 50,000 per violation and a discretionary 45-day cure period; in answer to an alleged violation, a company could show controls aligned with the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and ISO 42001. A minor could have recovered up to USD 10,000 in damages from a chatbot platform. A bill with the same title and the same bill description, SB 2-D (US-FL-04), was filed by Sen. Brodeur in 2026 Special Session D on 24 Apr 2026, passed the Senate 37-1 on 28 Apr 2026 and died in the House Information Technology Budget & Policy Subcommittee on 29 Apr 2026.
