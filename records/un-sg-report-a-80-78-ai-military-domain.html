Summary

First Secretary-General's report on AI in the military domain, requested by resolution 79/239 and focused on areas other than lethal autonomous weapons. The 151-page report consolidates submissions from 31 states, the EU, international organisations, the ICRC, civil society, academia and industry, catalogues normative proposals and summarises views on next steps. The Secretary-General names human responsibility and accountability as the chief challenge, calls machines with the discretion to take human lives 'politically unacceptable and morally repugnant', urges that nuclear-use decisions be made by humans, and warns that AI can lower barriers to chemical and biological weapons.

Status notes

Dated 5 June 2025 and reissued for technical reasons on 4 February 2026. States whose submissions are annexed include Argentina, Austria, Chile, China, Egypt, El Salvador, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, India, Indonesia, Iran, Israel, Italy, Japan, Lithuania, Mexico, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Pakistan, Peru, the Republic of Korea, the Russian Federation, Serbia, Singapore, Spain, Switzerland, Ukraine and the United Kingdom; the United States did not submit. Resolution 80/58 (December 2025) 'notes' the report and uses it as the basis for the 2026 Geneva informal exchanges, and the REAIM 2026 Pathways to Action also cites it. The Secretary-General's annual science-and-technology report (A/80/237) is a companion document. Submissions in the annex are in Chinese, English, French, Russian and Spanish.
