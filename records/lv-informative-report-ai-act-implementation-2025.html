Summary

Latvia's institutional plan for applying the AI Act directly, without a standalone AI law. Examined by the Cabinet on 25 February 2025, the report describes the Act's requirements, the actions public authorities must take and the legal changes and resources needed. It designates VARAM as the authority responsible for the Act overall and for cooperation with the European Commission, the Ministry of Economics as notifying authority and the Ombudsman as fundamental-rights authority, and names twelve market surveillance authorities led by the Consumer Rights Protection Centre, with the State Data Inspectorate for prohibited practices and high-risk systems and the Bank of Latvia for finance.

Status notes

The Commission's list of single points of contact (7 September 2026) names the Consumer Rights Protection Centre (PTAC) for Latvia without a pending mark. The financial-sector designation was legislated in the Financial Market Digital Operational Resilience and AI Use Law (LV-03), and the AI Centre Law (LV-01) provides the regulatory sandbox. A 2025 government review of a draft amendment to the Ombudsman Law noted that PTAC's market surveillance competence under the AI Act covers only AI embedded in physical products, not stand-alone software. No general AI Act implementing law or penalty regime outside the financial sector was found by October 2026.
