Summary

Comprehensive AI bill modelled on the EU's risk-based approach. As reported, providers would carry out a preliminary evaluation to classify a system's risk level before placing it on the market; applications with excessive risk would be prohibited, high-risk and government AI systems would face stricter controls and an algorithmic impact assessment procedure, and transparency and bias-reduction measures would apply. Affected persons would have rights to prior notice of interaction with AI, explanation of automated decisions, challenge of decisions with significant effects, human involvement, non-discrimination and data protection. The bill was referred to seven Senate committees.

Status notes

Presented in the Senate in May 2025 (a co-sponsor dates the presentation to 5 May 2025; the press reported its entry on 13 May 2025). A Senate public hearing followed, at which the ICT ministry (MITIC) recommended passing a personal-data-protection law first and proposed a regulatory sandbox; the bill remained under committee analysis. A separate Chamber of Deputies initiative on promoting AI for development was heard in June 2025 and is not recorded. The bill's SILpy file number was not identified.
