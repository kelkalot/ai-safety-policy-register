Summary

Second General Assembly resolution on AI in the military domain, following resolution 79/239. It reaffirms that international law, including the UN Charter, humanitarian law and human rights law, applies throughout the AI life cycle, notes the Secretary-General's report A/80/78, encourages national, regional and global efforts and UN-facilitated knowledge exchanges, and resolves to bridge divides between countries. It decides to convene informal exchanges in Geneva as a three-day meeting in 2026, open to all states, international organisations, the ICRC, academia, civil society and industry, with UNODA support and a factual summary to the First Committee. Civilian AI is excluded.

Status notes

Adopted at the 52nd plenary meeting on 1 December 2025 on the recommendation of the First Committee (A/80/534, para. 7; draft A/C.1/80/L.46); the document is dated 5 December 2025, which some sources quote as the adoption date. The recorded plenary vote is reported as 167 in favour, 5 against (Burundi, DPRK, Israel, Russian Federation, United States) and 5 abstentions (Argentina, Belarus, Iran, Nicaragua, Saudi Arabia); the First Committee vote on 6 November 2025 was reported as 166-5-5. The United States, which supported 79/239, voted against this text, reportedly objecting that it risked a centralised global governance regime for a critical technology. The informal exchanges mandated by paragraphs 9-10 were held in Geneva on 15-17 June 2026 under UNODA auspices. The preamble also notes Security Council meetings on AI held on 18 July 2023, 19 December 2024, 4 April 2025 and 24 September 2025.
