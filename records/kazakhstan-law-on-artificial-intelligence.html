Summary

Central Asia's first comprehensive AI law. It defines AI systems as objects of informatisation and sets principles including transparency and explainability, priority of human well-being, freedom of human decision-making, safety and security, and responsibility and controllability. It bans creating or operating AI systems with certain capabilities, e.g. subliminal or manipulative techniques and unlawful personal-data processing. It also requires goods, works and services created with AI to be labelled, places risk-management and safety duties on owners and holders, and provides a legal basis for a national AI platform.

Status notes

Signed on 17 Nov 2025, together with a companion law amending other legislative acts on AI and digitalisation. That companion law aligns existing laws with the AI Law and also covers digital assets, personal-data consent and information security. The Akorda notice does not give the law's number or entry-into-force date. Entry into force is reportedly 60 days after official publication (around Jan 2026), but this was not confirmed, so date_in_force is null. Status 'In force' assumes that commencement period has passed. Kazakhstan also created a Ministry of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Development in 2025 (referenced on primeminister.kz). The official legal database (adilet.zan.kz) requires JavaScript and could not be fetched. Uzbekistan and other Central Asian states were not covered.
