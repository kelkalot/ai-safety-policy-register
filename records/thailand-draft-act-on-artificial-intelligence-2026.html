Summary

Thailand's first draft general AI statute, released by ETDA for public hearing in July 2026 after the June 2025 'Draft Principles of the Law on Artificial Intelligence'. It covers AI affecting people in Thailand wherever operated, requires foreign providers to appoint a local representative, and sets three tiers: prohibited AI (subliminal manipulation, unfair broad-scale discrimination), high-risk AI designated by royal decree, and systems needing notification, registration or licensing. High-risk deployers must run risk management, assign oversight staff, keep logs and report unforeseen risks. It adds marking of AI media, joint liability and fines of THB 1-5 million.

Status notes

Sources differ on the hearing window: Tilleke & Gibbins report release on 2 July 2026 with about 30 days of comments; Rajah & Tann report 9 July to 14 August 2026. The hearing was hosted on the central law-consultation system (law.go.th), with comments to MDES. On 1 August 2026 Thai PBS Policy Watch reported that the hearing had concluded and that the next steps are review by the Council of State, Cabinet approval and submission to the House, with a government target of entering the legislative process by September 2026. The draft follows ETDA's consultation of 11-24 June 2025 on the '(Draft) Principles of the Law on Artificial Intelligence', which ETDA described as Thailand's first general AI law: risk-based supervision focused on high-risk uses affecting rights, freedoms or safety, a mix of soft and hard law, sector regulators empowered to issue controls or incentives, and promotion measures such as a text-and-data-mining exception and a regulatory sandbox. It succeeds the 2022-2023 ONDE draft Royal Decree on business operations using AI and the ETDA draft AI innovation promotion act, neither of which was enacted. Core provisions would apply on publication, with risk-control and incident provisions 180 days later.
