Summary

Non-binding guidelines that define trustworthy AI as lawful, ethical and robust. Founded on fundamental rights, they set four ethical principles (respect for human autonomy, prevention of harm, fairness, explicability) and seven key requirements: human agency and oversight, technical robustness and safety, privacy and data governance, transparency, diversity, non-discrimination and fairness, societal and environmental well-being, and accountability. An assessment list operationalises them, and the guidelines name critical concerns such as AI identification of individuals, covert AI systems, citizen scoring and lethal autonomous weapons.

Status notes

Written by the High-Level Expert Group on Artificial Intelligence (AI HLEG), an independent group of 52 members that the European Commission set up in June 2018, as the first of its two mandated deliverables (the other being policy and investment recommendations). The document states that its views are those of the AI HLEG and not an official position of the Commission. Made public on 8 Apr 2019, revised after a first draft of 18 Dec 2018 drew feedback from more than 500 contributors. Where an AI system poses risks to fundamental rights, a fundamental rights impact assessment should be done before development. The assessment list in Chapter III was piloted from 26 Jun to 1 Dec 2019; the AI HLEG then presented the final Assessment List for Trustworthy AI (ALTAI), a self-assessment checklist, in July 2020 (made public on 17 Jul 2020). The guidelines are available in 23 EU languages, with a separate AI HLEG document defining AI for their purpose.
