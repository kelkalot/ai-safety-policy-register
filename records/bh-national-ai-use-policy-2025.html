Summary

Government-wide policy that all Bahraini government entities must follow when adopting AI. Eleven principles include human decision-making (final decisions on important matters stay under human control), safety and prevention of harm, fairness, transparency and explainability, accountability, integrity and non-fabrication of data, privacy, and reliability and security of systems. Four pillars require compliance with data-protection, state-documents, cybercrime and open-data rules and the GCC AI ethics guideline; adoption of AI only in coordination with iGA, with regular audits and responsibility resting with officials rather than machines; staff training; and cooperation.

Status notes

Approved by the Ministerial Committee for Information and Communication Technology on 20 May 2025, from which date government entities are required to comply; publicly launched by iGA on 27 July 2025 together with Bahrain's adoption of the GCC Guiding Manual on the Ethics of AI Use (issued by the GCC Ministerial Committee for eGovernment). The document calls itself the 'General Policy for the Use of Artificial Intelligence'; iGA's publications list and press release call it the 'National Policy for the Use of Artificial Intelligence' (file dated 30 July 2025). It is an administrative policy binding government entities, not legislation, and contains no risk-tier system or penalties. Separately, the Shura Council approved in April 2024 a members' proposal for a 38-article law regulating AI (penalties, an AI unit, compensation for harm); no official record of its subsequent passage through the Council of Representatives was found.
