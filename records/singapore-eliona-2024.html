Summary

During an election period (writ to close of polling), it bans publishing, boosting, sharing or reposting online election advertising that is digitally generated or manipulated (AI deepfakes or conventional edits) and realistically shows a candidate saying or doing something they did not. Returning Officers can issue corrective directions. Individuals face fines up to S$1,000 and/or 12 months' jail; social media services that fail to comply face fines up to S$1 million.

Status notes

First reading 9 Sep 2024; second reading and passage 15 Oct 2024. It amends the Parliamentary Elections Act 1954 and the Presidential Elections Act 1991. The prohibition applies only during election periods. Per law-firm reports, it was first applied in the 2025 General Election (writ issued 15 Apr 2025, polling 3 May 2025). The Act's gazette number and formal commencement date were not confirmed from an official source, so date_in_force is left null. IMDA also planned a Code of Practice requiring social media services to address manipulated content. Singapore's separate Online Safety (Relief and Accountability) Act 2025 (Online Safety Commission operational from 29 Jun 2026) addresses online harms more broadly and is not recorded here because it is not AI-specific.
