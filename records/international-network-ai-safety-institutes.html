Summary

All 10 founding members agreed a joint mission statement. It sets the Network up as a forum to build a common technical understanding of AI safety risks and mitigations, to support interoperable principles and best practices, and to encourage a shared global approach to AI safety. Its work centres on the science of AI testing and evaluation.

Status notes

Launched 20 Nov 2024 in San Francisco with the US as inaugural chair. Renamed 'International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science', confirmed at the Network's meeting in San Diego alongside NeurIPS; the UK announced this on 9 Dec 2025 and took on a 'Network Coordinator' role. The US now takes part through NIST's Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI), the renamed US AI Safety Institute. On 13 Feb 2026 NIST published the Network's consensus practices and open questions on automated AI evaluations. The G7 Digital & Technology Ministerial Declaration (May 2026) tasks France with working with the Network and the OECD on comparable risk-assessment frameworks.
