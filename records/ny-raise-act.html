Summary

Frontier-AI safety law modelled on, but stricter than, California SB 53. Developers of frontier models (above 10^26 operations) must publish safety frameworks and transparency reports, and report critical safety incidents within 72 hours (24 hours for imminent risk of death or serious injury). Large frontier developers (over $500M revenue) must file disclosure statements, pay assessments and submit confidential catastrophic-risk summaries. A new DFS oversight office supervises compliance, and the Attorney General can seek penalties of up to $1M for a first violation and $3M for later ones.

Status notes

Passed June 2025 and signed 19 Dec 2025 as Chapter 699 of 2025 (approval memo 76) on the understanding that chapter amendments would follow. The chapter amendment S8828 passed the Senate 28 Jan 2026 and the Assembly 11 Mar 2026 (substituted for A9449), and was signed 27 Mar 2026 as Chapter 96 of 2026. It replaced the original compute-spend test with the $500M revenue test, cut penalties from $10M/$30M to $1M/$3M, and moved the effective date to 1 Jan 2027. The DFS office's annual reports begin 1 Jan 2028. No private right of action. A federal legal challenge was not identified as of Oct 2026, though the law is cited as a likely EO 14365 target and the federal FRONTIER Act would preempt it.
