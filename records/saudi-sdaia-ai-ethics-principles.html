Summary

Saudi Arabia's national AI ethics framework, issued by SDAIA to support the Kingdom's AI strategies, govern data and AI models so as to limit the negative effects of AI systems, and help entities adopt ethics standards when building AI solutions. It sets out seven principles (fairness, privacy and security, humanity, social and environmental benefits, reliability and safety, transparency and explainability, and accountability and responsibility) with associated controls across the AI system life cycle, including continuous monitoring, performance reporting, predefined triggers and human oversight in the deploy-and-monitor stage, and assigns roles and responsibilities, with SDAIA developing and updating the principles, preparing national guides and standards, and monitoring compliance.

Status notes

The PDF at the official URL carries the year 2025 on its cover and 'Version Number 1' in its document-control table; no month is given, so date_published is 2025. Law-firm commentaries describe an earlier version 2.0 of September 2023 (which introduced risk tiers from low to unacceptable) and a version 1.0 of 2022; the 2025 edition does not state how it relates to that numbering. Commentators disagree on whether the principles are binding on the entities in scope; the document itself describes SDAIA as monitoring compliance, so it is recorded here as non-binding guidance pending clarification. SDAIA's generative AI guidelines (SA-02), its deepfakes guidelines (SA-03) and CST's draft Global AI Hub Law (SA-04) are recorded separately.
