Summary

Consensus statement of the G20 Innovation Ministerial (Chapel Hill, 1-2 September 2026), the first AI-related outcome of the US presidency. It frames emerging technologies as drivers of prosperity when deployed 'in a secure and reliable manner', endorses sector-specific, risk-based approaches where appropriate and asks members to preserve national sovereignty in technology governance. Six pillars cover pro-innovation policy, AI in public services, workforce, IP policies for AI, 'AI for standards and standards for AI' and industrial innovation. The Carolina Principles favour secure real-world testing and existing sector regulation, with new rules only for novel issues.

Status notes

The Commerce Department lists the African Union, Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, the European Union, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Poland, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Korea, Türkiye and the United Kingdom as present; it describes the statement as a consensus text but does not list signatories per document. Compared with the Johannesburg declaration's list of safety requirements, the statement is innovation-led and contains no provisions on frontier-model risk or safety institutes. The G20 Leaders' Summit of the US presidency is scheduled for 14-15 December 2026 in Miami, so no 2026 leaders' AI outcome exists yet. The same PDF is also hosted by Japan's Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications.
