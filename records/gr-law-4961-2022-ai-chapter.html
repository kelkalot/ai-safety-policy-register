Summary

Greece's first AI-specific statutory provisions (Articles 3-14). Public-sector bodies may use AI systems to take or support decisions affecting the rights of persons only where a specific law provides for it with safeguards; defence, public-order and intelligence bodies are excluded. Before operating such a system a body must carry out an algorithmic impact assessment covering purpose, capabilities, decision types, data, risks to rights and expected benefit. Further articles set transparency duties, obligations of AI contractors, a public-sector AI register, an information duty for private employers whose AI affects hiring or working conditions, and an AI Observatory.

Status notes

Signed on 26 July 2022 and published on 27 July 2022; the AI chapter applied from 1 January 2023 (Article 116(2)). The rest of the law covers cybersecurity, Internet of Things devices, unmanned systems and other digital-governance matters. Chapter B of Part A was repealed by Article 26 of Law 5321/2026 with effect from 20 July 2026, which carried the public-sector AI register and the AI Observatory into the new law and set up the AI Act supervisory structure.
