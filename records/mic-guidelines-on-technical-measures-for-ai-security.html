Summary

Guidelines of the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications on technical measures that AI developers and AI providers can take to secure AI, mainly large language models and systems that contain them. Building on the AI Guidelines for Business and J-AISI's evaluation guide, they focus on direct and indirect prompt injection and denial-of-service attacks, and also cover data poisoning, attacks through tampered models and model extraction. Measures include training on safety standards, system prompts, guardrails that check inputs, outputs and external data, and permission management for orchestrators and RAG, with red teaming to confirm that they work. AI agents are excluded for now.

Status notes

A draft was open for public comment from 26 Dec 2025 to 29 Jan 2026 and drew 52 comments; MIC published them with its views alongside the final guidelines and a separate annex of supporting material. The guidelines follow the conclusions, reached in December 2025, of the AI Security Subcommittee that MIC convened from September 2025 under its Cybersecurity Task Force. They state that they do not decide who is responsible for a threat, and MIC intends to supplement them as new threats emerge, for example from vision language models, AI agents and MCP.
