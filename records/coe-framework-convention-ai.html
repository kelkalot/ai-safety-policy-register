Summary

The first legally binding international treaty on AI. Parties must ensure that activities across the AI lifecycle are consistent with human rights, democracy and the rule of law, through risk and impact assessment, transparency, oversight, accountability and remedies. It applies fully to the public sector; for the private sector, Parties choose how to address risks.

Status notes

Adopted by the Committee of Ministers on 17 May 2024 and opened for signature in Vilnius on 5 Sep 2024. It enters into force on the first day of the month after a three-month period starting from the fifth ratification, of which at least three must be CoE member states. The CoE page lists 21 signatories: Albania, Andorra, Armenia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Georgia, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Norway, Republic of Moldova, San Marino, Switzerland, Ukraine, UK, Canada, EU, Israel, Japan, USA and Uruguay. The EU ratified on 15 May 2026 at the 135th Committee of Ministers session in Chișinău. As of Oct 2026 the CoE AI page lists only the EU as a Party and no entry into force has been announced. A June 2026 CDNET report urges ratification 'with a view to achieving early entry into force'. The Steering Committee for New and Emerging Digital Technologies (CDNET), which replaced the CAI, is the treaty's custodian; first plenary 15-17 Apr 2026, second plenary 21-23 Oct 2026.
