Laws of Florida ch. 2024-118 (CS/CS/SB 1680, 2024); creates ss. 282.802 and 827.072, F.S.

Summary

Creates the Government Technology Modernization Council in the Department of Management Services, chaired by the Lieutenant Governor, to study and monitor new technologies and recommend how to procure and regulate them. It must assess reforms and a state code of ethics for AI systems in state government and the effect of automated decision systems on residents' rights, evaluate common standards for AI safety and security, including provenance disclosure for generative AI images and audio, and determine how bad actors exploit AI. The law also makes knowingly possessing or intentionally viewing, or intentionally creating, generated child pornography a third-degree felony.

Status notes

Passed the Senate 40-0 on 28 Feb 2024 and the House 114-0 on 6 Mar 2024; approved by the Governor on 26 Apr 2024 and effective 1 Jul 2024. The council (s. 282.802, F.S.) comprises the Lieutenant Governor as chair, the state chief information officer, the Secretaries of Commerce, Health Care Administration and Transportation, the executive director of the Department of Law Enforcement, five experts in fields including AI, machine learning and constitutional law, and one member of each legislative chamber; it meets at least quarterly, once jointly with the Florida Cybersecurity Advisory Council, and its duties also include data modernisation, AI use by government and business, and digital media literacy curricula. By 31 Dec each year from 2024 it recommends legislation to modernise government technology, including policies to protect Floridians from bad actors who use AI. Generated child pornography (s. 827.072, F.S.) is an image created, altered, adapted or modified by electronic, mechanical or other computer-generated means to portray a fictitious person whom a reasonable person would regard as a real person under 18 engaged in sexual conduct; the law also requires courts in criminal proceedings to deny defendants' requests to copy such material when the state attorney makes it reasonably available (s. 92.561). In 2026, ch. 2026-18 and ch. 2026-20 amended s. 827.072, renaming the material generated child sexual abuse material, making its creation a second-degree felony and adding a second-degree felony for transmitting it; ch. 2026-20 was approved on 31 Mar 2026 and took effect on 1 Jul 2026. Ch. 2026-66 amended s. 282.802, changing the cross-reference that defines foreign countries of concern.
