Summary

Policy guide for Egypt's National AI Governance Framework, approved for publication in March 2026. It adopts a 'State as Orchestrator' model with central policy-setting by the Council and oversight by sectoral regulators, and sorts AI systems into four tiers: prohibited (unacceptable risk, e.g. social scoring, exploitation of children, manipulation), high-risk (e.g. critical infrastructure, biometrics) subject to a mandatory 'dual-check' of ethical impact assessment plus technical audit, limited-risk (chatbots, deepfakes) subject to transparency and labelling, and minimal-risk under voluntary codes; general-purpose AI with systemic impact counts as high-risk. It also plans an AI audit lab.

Status notes

The guide's document-control table records a version 1.0 for public consultation (January 2026), integration of feedback (February 2026) and authorisation by the Minister of Communications and Information Technology as Council chair on 15 March 2026 (Edition 2.0, 'Approved for Publication'). Trackers report the release of the guide, the Trustworthy and Responsible AI guidelines and the generative AI guidelines on 14 March 2026, and MCIT's AI page lists a March 2026 news item on the Council adopting the governance framework. The companion 'Egypt National Guidelines for Trustworthy and Responsible AI' (Edition 2.0, authorised 25 March 2026; secondary_url) is described as Product 1 of the framework and its operational manual; it organises obligations around institutional governance, AI life-cycle governance (impact assessments, post-deployment monitoring and audit, incident response), stakeholder engagement, and society and sustainability. It is recorded with the guide rather than as a separate entry.
