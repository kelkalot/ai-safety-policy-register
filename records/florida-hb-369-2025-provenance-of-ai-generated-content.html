Summary

Bill that would have required developers of publicly available generative AI tools to offer a tool letting users apply provenance data to synthetic content made with the developer's tool, or to apply such data themselves where technically feasible, and to make a free provenance reader available. Provenance data, following widely adopted standards, would show whether visual content is synthetic and name the developer, without personally identifying information. Social media platforms would have had to retain available provenance data and make it accessible to users. Violations would have been unfair or deceptive practices, enforced only by the Attorney General after a 30-day cure period.

Status notes

Filed 4 Feb 2025. Reported favourably with committee substitutes by the Information Technology Budget & Policy Subcommittee (17-0, 19 Mar 2025), the Economic Infrastructure Subcommittee (16-0, 31 Mar 2025) and the Commerce Committee (25-0, 15 Apr 2025). The first substitute dropped provisions of the filed version on disclaimers on election materials, a pilot programme at the Division of Emergency Management and capture devices; the Commerce Committee replaced 'provider' with 'developer', removed audio from 'synthetic content' and excluded services that exclusively provide video game experiences. The bill would have created s. 501.9741, F.S., with an effective date of 1 Jul 2026. Placed on the House Second Reading Calendar, temporarily postponed on 23 Apr 2025, indefinitely postponed and withdrawn from consideration on 3 May 2025, and died on the Second Reading Calendar on 16 Jun 2025. The similar Senate bill SB 702 (Burgess) died in the Ethics and Elections Committee.
