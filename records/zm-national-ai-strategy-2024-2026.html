Summary

Zambia's first national AI strategy, developed with support from Finland and the Tony Blair Institute and launched in Lusaka on 21 November 2024. It sets guiding principles of fairness, transparency, accountability, data privacy, inclusivity and sustainability aligned with the AU Continental AI Strategy, UNESCO's Recommendation and the OECD AI Principles, and prioritises a policy and regulatory framework for ethical and responsible AI alongside skills, infrastructure, research and sectoral adoption. Governance measures include a National AI Council, technical working groups, AI ethics guidelines, extending the regulator ZICTA's mandate to AI, regulatory sandboxes, and risk management.

Status notes

The cover reads 'National Artificial Intelligence Strategy 2024-2026' and the foreword refers to the 2024-2026 strategy, but the running page header says '2025-2027' and OECD.AI lists it under 2025-2027. The ministry announced in July 2024 that the strategy would be launched on Independence Day (24 October 2024); its release dated 21 November 2024 (published 25 November) reports the launch that day in Lusaka. The PDF was uploaded to mots.gov.zm in February 2025 and is byte-identical to the copy hosted by OECD.AI.
