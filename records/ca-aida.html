Bill C-27 (44th Parliament, 1st Session)

Summary

Would have created Canada's first federal AI statute, imposing risk-based obligations on 'high-impact' AI systems (risk assessment and mitigation, monitoring, record-keeping, transparency, notification of material harm), an AI and Data Commissioner, and offences for deploying AI that causes serious harm. It never passed committee stage and died when Parliament was prorogued.

Status notes

Introduced 16 June 2022 (first reading); referred to the Standing Committee on Industry and Technology 24 April 2023; last committee meeting 26 Sept 2024; died on the Order Paper when the 1st Session of the 44th Parliament was prorogued on 6 Jan 2025. No AIDA successor as of Oct 2026: the June 2026 National AI Strategy relies on privacy and online-safety law modernisation instead. Bill C-36 (Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act, first reading 15 June 2026, sponsored by the Minister of AI and Digital Innovation) re-enacts only the privacy part of C-27 (incl. automated-decision-system disclosure) and is excluded here as a general privacy law. AI Minister Evan Solomon said in late Sept 2026 that AI deployment needs regulation but gave no details (press report, not an official source).
