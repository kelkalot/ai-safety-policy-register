Summary

Thailand's AI ethics principles and guidelines, prepared under the Ministry of Digital Economy and Society. They guide AI researchers, designers, developers and service providers in their work, let service recipients know their rights and the risks of AI services, and give state agencies and AI regulators a basis for promoting, supporting and supervising AI, so AI is reliable and secure and benefits people, society and the environment with transparency, inclusiveness and fairness, in line with law, ethics and human rights. After reviewing foreign and corporate frameworks, it sets six principles with guidance under each for state agencies and regulators, developers and providers, and users.

Status notes

The 46-page document hosted by ETDA is undated. Its cover title is in English only; in its Thai text the document calls itself 'เอกสารหลักการและแนวทางจริยธรรมปัญญาประดิษฐ์' (AI ethics principles and guidelines document). The most recent sources it cites date from 2019, and it refers to the Ministry's strategy for B.E. 2563-2567 (2020-2024). Its six principles are Competitiveness and Sustainability Development; Laws, Ethics and International Standards; Transparency and Accountability; Security and Privacy; Fairness; and Reliability. They were later carried into ETDA's 2023 AI Governance Guideline for Executives. Its review covers OECD, Council of Europe, European Commission, Smart Dubai, Singapore PDPC, Microsoft and Beijing AI principles. The guideline has no enforcement mechanism; Thailand's binding AI framework is still at the stage of the 2026 draft Act on Artificial Intelligence.
