Summary

SB 1119, 'Adam's Law', named after Adam Raine, expands California's child-safety rules for companion chatbots, building on SB 243. It requires age assurance, risk assessments before a new or substantially modified companion chatbot is released, in-app crisis referrals and notice to a parent of a credible threat of self-harm, parent-controlled default settings such as time limits, reasonable measures against harmful outputs such as self-harm, sexually explicit or emotionally manipulative content, an incident reporting mechanism overseen by the Attorney General, limits on targeted advertising to children, and independent compliance audits reported to the Attorney General.

Status notes

Passed the Legislature on 31 Aug 2026 (Senate 39-0, Assembly 64-4) and signed 10 Sep 2026 (Padilla; Wicks; Bauer-Kahan) in a 13-bill child online-safety package that also included SB 867 (toys: companion chatbots), AB 1709 (covered platforms: age restriction) and SB 1276 (crimes: sexual exploitation of a child). It builds on SB 243 (2025), uses the age-bracket signal of AB 1043 (2025) and draws on provisions of AB 1064 (2025) on harmful outputs. It also gives families harmed by violations of its crisis-referral, parental-notice, default-protection and harmful-output provisions a private right of redress.
