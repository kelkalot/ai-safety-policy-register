    What is in the register

    The register covers official instruments on AI safety and AI regulation issued by governments, legislatures, regulators and intergovernmental bodies. It includes:

    • AI-specific laws and implementing regulations, including major sub-national laws such as US state statutes.
    • Executive orders, decrees and cabinet decisions on AI.
    • Government safety and risk-management frameworks, guidelines, voluntary codes and technical standards.
    • Founding mandates of AI safety and AI security institutes.
    • Flagship national AI strategies, only where they contain substantial safety or risk-governance content.
    • Intergovernmental declarations, treaties, resolutions and government-commissioned scientific reports.
    • Major pending bills, and revoked, lapsed or superseded instruments, kept for context and labelled as such.

    It leaves out R&D or industrial strategies without safety content, general privacy laws that are not AI-specific, minor sectoral circulars, and documents written by companies, think tanks or the press.

    How each record was verified

    Every record was researched on 11 or 12 October 2026 against an official primary source: a government or legislature website, an official gazette, EUR-Lex, or the site of the intergovernmental body. The official page was fetched to confirm the title, date and status. The level shows how strong that confirmation is.

    A

    Official source fetched. The official URL on the record loaded, and the title, date and status were confirmed on it.

    B

    Alternative official source. The primary page blocked automated access, so the details were confirmed on another official page, such as a ministry press release, a translation hosted by a government, or the OECD.AI entry.

    C

    Not confirmed on an official page. No official copy could be fetched. Details rest on news reports or official statements about the document. Re-check these before citing them.

    Six records with surprising 2026 developments got a second, independent fetch of their official page. Those records show a second-check note in their verification block.

    This is an AI-based extraction. The research, the summaries, the status notes and the topic tags were produced by an AI agent from the pages it could fetch, following the method documented in AGENTS.md in the repository. No human has reviewed every record. The verification level says what was confirmed on an official page; treat level C records with caution and re-check any record before relying on it. Anyone can extend or re-verify the register by running another agent pass with the same method; AGENTS.md explains how.

    Dates use ISO format; a date is shortened to YYYY-MM or YYYY when the source gives no exact day. date_published is the adoption, signature or issue date. date_in_force is the entry-into-force or effective date. Phased application dates are listed in each record's notes.

    Local copies of sources

    Every source is also stored on the machine that hosts the register, so the register does not depend on links that can move or disappear. tools/archive.py downloads the official URL, up to two PDFs that page links to on the same official site (usually the legal text), and the secondary URL. Each file is saved under archive/<ID>/<date>/ with its SHA-256 hash, the UTC time it was retrieved, the final URL after redirects and the HTTP status. Each dated folder also holds a SHA256SUMS file, so sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS checks the copies independently.

    The archiver checks robots.txt before downloading and waits between requests to the same site. Re-running it keeps unchanged files once, stores a changed source as a new dated snapshot, and keeps an earlier copy if the source later disappears. python3 tools/archive.py --verify re-hashes every local file and flags any that are missing or have been modified. Setup and update steps are in README.md.

    Records to re-check first

      Known coverage gaps

      A first research pass hit a search limit and listed its gaps here. Eleven agent passes on 11 and 12 October 2026, following AGENTS.md, then extended the register from 208 to 340 records; each carries its own verification level, and the content checks in docs/ were applied. Every jurisdiction in the list below was searched; what remains is either not adopted yet or sits behind an official site that refuses automated access. In the second case the record exists at level B or C and says so.

      • Europe: Belgium and Estonia have no AI Act implementing instrument yet; Austria has not drafted one; Croatia's and Lithuania's are at the draft stage behind unreachable portals; Romania's strategy text (legislatie.just.ro) could not be read; Iceland's EEA incorporation is pending; Ukraine's AI law exists only as an announcement; Cyprus' strategy consultation and Slovakia's deepfake bill were not located on official pages. The Dutch and Czech official pages refuse automated copies.
      • Asia: South Korea's two MSIT notifications in final form; India's March 2024 MeitY advisories, no longer hosted on the ministry site; the Philippine DTI and DOST strategies and Senate AI bills, whose official sites block access; Thailand's 2024 generative AI guideline and the official hearing page of its draft AI Act (the draft is recorded at level C); Sri Lanka, Mongolia, Tajikistan, Armenia and Georgia, where nothing adopted was found.
      • Middle East & Africa: Kuwait's draft strategy; Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria, Côte d'Ivoire, Uganda, Tanzania, Namibia and Cameroon, whose strategies or bills are not yet adopted or published; Qatar's NCSA secure-AI guidelines, Türkiye's action plan, Ghana's emerging-technologies bill. Ethiopia's, Zambia's and Kenya's sites block automated copies.
      • Americas: official records for Nevada's AB 406, Oregon's SB 1546, Idaho's SB 1297, Rhode Island's S 2195 and Paraguay's AI bill (their hosts refuse automated access, so these records are level C or rest on secondary copies); Tennessee, Arizona, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania and Michigan chatbot and deepfake measures and the 2026 laws of Colorado, New York, Utah and Missouri (not yet researched or official hosts blocked); Costa Rica, Panama, the Dominican Republic, Guatemala and Trinidad and Tobago (sites unreachable or nothing adopted).
      • International: no dedicated G7 Evian AI statement exists; the G20 2026 leaders' outcome is due in December 2026; the UN document server and brics.br block automated copies, so those records hold secondary or mirror copies.

      Field reference

      FieldMeaning
      idRegister ID: jurisdiction code plus sequence, e.g. EU-01, US-CA-03.
      title / short_titleOfficial title (official English title where one exists) and common short name.
      original_titleTitle in the original language, where it is not English.
      jurisdiction / levelIssuing country, bloc or body; International, Supranational, National or Sub-national.
      instrument_typeLaw, regulation, executive order, guidelines, voluntary code, standard, institute mandate, declaration, treaty, resolution, bill, strategy or report. Binding internal government directives (OMB memoranda, Treasury Board directives, APS policies) are typed as regulation / implementing rule.
      legal_force / statusBinding, non-binding or proposed; current status as of the snapshot.
      date_*Published or adopted, in force, and last updated (amendment, new version, delay or status change). ISO dates; YYYY-MM or YYYY when the source gives no exact day. The published date is the date of the act itself (adoption, signature or issue); the gazette or Official Journal date, where different, is in the notes.
      official_identifierThe citable number: CELEX or OJ reference, EO number, CETS number, UN symbol, bill number.
      official_url / secondary_urlPrimary official source, and a second authoritative source where useful.
      verificationLevel (A/B/C), date checked, method, what was confirmed, and any second check.
